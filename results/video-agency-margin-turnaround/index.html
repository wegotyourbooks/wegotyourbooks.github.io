The situation

The business installs video production systems for B2B marketing teams. When we first talked, in February 2026, it was growing and the growth was the problem.

He had a tax accountant and a bookkeeper, both in what he called siloed operations. The bookkeeper was doing "the bare minimum," and reporting that was technically in scope arrived inconsistently "despite prodding."

What he wanted wasn't more bookkeeping: "we want it to be even more ruthless and dialed in so we can make more informed decisions." And the constraint behind it, said plainly: "We're not financial people."

Why it had become urgent

"It's been a huge pain point these last few months," he said, "and not good for longevity, honestly."

What we did

Modelled three scenarios against a margin target

The owner had a target operating margin he wanted by year end. We built three paths to test whether it was reachable.

  1. 1

    Baseline

    Change nothing, roll Q1 forward through the year.

  2. 2

    Growth only

    Hold the cost structure, grow revenue at the historical 15% to 20%.

  3. 3

    Blended

    Modest revenue growth plus real cost reduction. The only one that got there, and the one the business ran.

A forecast with a single number can't make that argument.

Rebuilt revenue reporting into five buckets

Revenue was arriving as one line, which made it impossible to see which offering carried margin. It reports in five now: legacy video production, event rentals, video install, video operator, and the historical course business.

The QuickBooks income categories were realigned to match how the owner already thought about his offers, including transactions invoiced through Stripe.

Handed over a pricing calculator and a list of clients to fix

The calculator is a Google Sheet he uses himself to model pricing on his hero offers, so the next price change is his call rather than ours.

Then the uncomfortable half: a list of recurring clients locked into older, low-margin offers. Knowing which accounts are underpriced is the prerequisite for repricing them.

Set an order of operations for every spare dollar

The sequence, written down

Cash reserve first, to two or three months of operating expenses. Owner pay after that. So the question "can I take money out yet" has an answer that doesn't change with mood.

Modelled the hiring gate and a cash target

Hiring gate thresholds, plus a goal of six or more months of operating cash on hand. And a maternity leave payroll structure at a reduced rate, because forecasting the real calendar beats forecasting a clean one.

Separated the building from the agency

A building with event rental income was mixed into the operating picture, which made both harder to read. It gets tracked as its own unit now, against its own break-even, and it's been running slightly above that line.

Two businesses with different economics, reported separately, so a soft month in one stops disguising a good month in the other.

Moved to async monthly, live quarterly

A Loom walkthrough of the numbers each month, with a live review call each quarter. Less meeting, same information.

Where it landed

First half of 2026

Revenue up 36% year over year. Contractor cost down 38% in a single month to its lowest level in twelve. Reporting now separates five revenue lines, so which offering carries the business is visible rather than inferred.

What hasn't landed yet

This is a turnaround in progress, and the owner is clearer about that than we would be on his behalf: "it's not a light switch that happens over a day," he said, "it's going to take weeks and months to really turn the tides."

What changed immediately was knowing which lever to pull. "I felt definitely a great deal of optimism seeing that like what is possible," he said, "and knowing like what are the exact action items."

On what the engagement is, in his words: "kind of like a fractional CFO ... or some kind of financial controller who's stepping in without having to add headcount."

Questions

The ones people actually ask on the first call

Why model three scenarios instead of one forecast?

Because the useful output was a decision, not a number. Two of the three paths did not reach the margin target, which made the case for the third on arithmetic rather than on opinion.

What does splitting revenue into five buckets change?

It shows which offering carries margin. With revenue arriving as a single line you can watch the business get bigger and still have no idea which work is worth doing more of.

Is this engagement a success story yet?

Yes, and it is not finished. Revenue growth and the contractor cost reduction are both real and measurable, and the owner knows which lever to pull next. Turning a margin around takes months rather than weeks, and we would rather say so.

Why separate the building from the agency in the reporting?

Different businesses, different economics. Blended, a soft month in one hides a good month in the other and neither number means much. Separated, the building tracks against its own break-even and the agency month reads clean.

What replaced the previous bookkeeper arrangement?

One engagement covering the monthly close, the dashboard, an annual budget and twice-yearly forecasts, with quarterly calls. The previous setup had a tax accountant and a bookkeeper working separately.
