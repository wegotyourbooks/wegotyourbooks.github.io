Profitability KPIs: read them together

One number on its own rarely tells you what to do. The three profitability KPIs explain each other.

  • Profit below its band, people cost above 40%: the agency is overstaffed for its revenue or underpriced for its work. The decision rule is to freeze hiring and look at pricing and utilization.
  • Profit in its band, revenue per employee falling: headcount is growing faster than revenue. The margin will follow unless pricing or utilization moves.
  • Profit above its band, utilization over 120% for months: the team is carrying more than it can hold. This is the case where the hiring gate can clear.
  • Profit healthy, months of cash low: the profit is sitting in receivables. Check AR days before anything else.

Profit, revenue per employee, payroll % and software % are the four the Agency Financial OS reviews every month, against the benchmarks above.

Three ways agency KPIs go wrong

  • Measured on books that haven't closed. A KPI is only as good as the month underneath it. Review after the close, on a set date, and the numbers stop moving after you've looked at them.
  • Contractors left out. People cost and revenue per employee both count contractors, as fractional FTEs for headcount. Leave them out and the team looks cheaper and more productive than it is.
  • Pass-throughs counted as revenue. Rebilled media inflates revenue, which flatters revenue per employee and drags margin percentages down. Track agency gross income instead when pass-throughs are large.

Each of these is a bookkeeping decision before it is a reporting one, which is why the KPIs start with how the books are set up.

Margin and utilization in one place

Margin lives in the accounting system and utilization lives in time tracking, which is why most agencies look at them separately. The two meet in one number: margin per project, built from tracked hours.

  1. 1

    Track time against clients and projects

    Every hour logged to the engagement it served, including non-billable time.

  2. 2

    Turn hours into cost

    Hours multiplied by each person's cost per hour gives the time cost of each engagement.

  3. 3

    Set cost against what was invoiced

    Invoiced amount minus time cost and fixed cost gives margin and margin percent for every engagement.

  4. 4

    Review it with the close

    Once a month, next to operating profit, so a low-margin client shows up while it can still be repriced.

Questions

The ones people actually ask on the first call

What are the most important financial KPIs for an agency?

Operating profit margin, people cost as a percentage of revenue, and revenue per employee. Together they show whether growth is making money. Cash and AR days come next, because they show whether profit is reaching the bank.

What is a good profit margin for an agency?

On the WGYB benchmark, operating profit after payroll and before owner distributions runs 10% to 15% at $300K to $600K in revenue, 15% to 20% at $600K to $1.2M, and 18% to 25% at $1.2M to $3M.

What's the difference between gross margin and operating margin?

Gross margin takes out only the cost of delivering client work: the team and contractors on it. Operating margin also takes out overhead, everything that runs the agency. Gross margin shows whether the work is priced right; operating margin shows whether the business is.

How often should an agency review its KPIs?

Profit, revenue per employee, payroll % and software % monthly, after the close. Owner pay and tax estimates quarterly. The hiring gate before any new role.

How do I track margin and utilization in one place?

Bring tracked hours and the accounting data together as margin per project: hours times cost per hour, set against what was invoiced. Review it monthly next to operating profit.

Where should agency KPIs live?

On a dashboard that reads from your accounting system, so the numbers update with every close and come from one source. Ours is built in Fathom on QuickBooks Online.
