The one metric agencies underuse

People cost as a percentage of revenue, with a rolling average beside it.

One client watched that line bend down about four points while the team cost held flat and revenue grew. Dollars up 13%, revenue up 22%. The chart is what made it obvious.

Four things clients asked us to add, and what got built

A service-line share chart

One agency's video work was running ahead of its target share of revenue and swinging month to month. The dashboard got a chart tracking that line as both dollars and a percentage, against the target.

A mini P&L for billable pass-throughs

Same agency: client travel, meals, advertising, billable and production expenses were all being spent and rebilled, and the books couldn't show whether the rebilling covered the spend. We consolidated all of it against the matching income, monthly and annual.

The aggregate came out positive, and wide enough that the owner decided to audit his own billing.

A gross-profit-only report for a manager

One owner wanted to put a team member on a gross profit margin KPI, but couldn't give him QuickBooks access without exposing owner pay. Locking down visibility isn't enough: anyone who sees the P&L can work backward.

The report stops at the gross profit line. A high-level PDF with a granular sheet underneath, monthly, so he can see how the number was built and nothing below it.

Per-project margin from tracked hours

For a project-based client, the monthly pack includes margin by client built from time entries rather than estimates: hours, time cost, fixed cost, invoiced amount, margin and margin percent, for every engagement.

The spread between the best and the worst was wide enough to change which work they chase. That engagement is written up here.

Questions

The ones people actually ask on the first call

What software do you build the dashboard in?

Fathom, reading from QuickBooks Online. QuickBooks stays the single source of truth and the dashboard is the reporting layer on top of it. You get your own login rather than a PDF.

How often does the dashboard update?

With each month-end close. On the Scale tier it is joined by a weekly financial report covering income, cost of goods sold, expenses and net income week by week.

Can the dashboard track metrics specific to my agency?

Yes, and the ones that get used usually do. Clients have added a service-line revenue share chart against a target, a consolidated view of billable pass-through spend against the income that covers it, and per-project margin built from tracked hours.

Can I give a team member access without showing them owner pay?

Not through permissions alone, because anyone who can see the P&L can work backward to owner compensation. The approach that works is a separate report that stops at the gross profit line, which is what we build when a manager owns a margin target.

Is the dashboard included at every tier?

It is included on Growth and Scale. On Starter it is a $200 a month add-on, because agencies under $500K usually need clean books and a clean tax handoff more than they need a reporting layer.
