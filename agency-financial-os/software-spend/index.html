How Much Should an Agency Spend on Software?

Use software to increase margins without letting tools quietly destroy profitability

Software should scale slower than revenue, faster than headcount, and never faster than profit.

Goal

Use software to increase delivery capacity and margins without letting tools quietly destroy profitability.

Benchmark: Software as % of Revenue

Includes all software costs: accounting, CRM, operations, project management, delivery tools, etc.

Branding & Creative Agencies

Brand strategy, design studios, video production, other creative production. Cost structure: labor heavy, tool light: few specialized platforms.

Recommended range: 2%–4% of revenue

SEO / Performance / Paid Media Agencies

SEO, PPC, analytics, CRO, marketing operations. Cost structure: software-heavy delivery: tools are part of cost of service and directly create client value.

Recommended range: 3%–6% of revenue

Once software exceeds that threshold, it almost always indicates: tool overlap, low adoption, individual-driven buying, or poor cancellation discipline.

Decision Rules
  • Budget Rule: Annual software budget = Revenue × target percentage. Divide by 12 for monthly cap.
  • New Tool Rule: Any new tool must replace an existing tool, increase revenue capacity, reduce headcount needs, or improve pricing power.
  • Cancellation Rule (Quarterly): Cancel tools used by less than 20% of the team, not tied to delivery or revenue, or duplicating existing functionality.
Tradeoffs

Higher software spend improves delivery efficiency and scalability, but lowers margins, increases fixed costs, and raises break-even point.

Worked example: a $1M creative agency with a team of 7

  • Target: 3%, the middle of the creative range
  • Annual budget: $1,000,000 × 3% = $30,000
  • Monthly cap: $30,000 ÷ 12 = $2,500, which matches the healthy row in the table above
  • Cancellation rule: cancel tools used by less than 20% of the team. With 7 people, 20% is 1.4, so a tool used by one person is a candidate

A team of 7 is the size of the Revenue Per Employee module's example agency. If spend runs over the cap, the module names the usual causes: tool overlap, low adoption, individual-driven buying, or poor cancellation discipline.

Where software sits on the P&L

Software that delivers client work, such as SEO, analytics or design tools, belongs in cost of delivery, next to the team that uses it. Software that runs the business, such as accounting, CRM and project management, sits in overhead. The benchmark counts both, but splitting them shows which part grows with clients and which part grows with headcount. Software should scale slower than revenue, faster than headcount, and never faster than profit.

Questions

How much should an agency spend on software?

On the WGYB benchmark, 2% to 4% of revenue for branding and creative agencies and 3% to 6% for SEO, performance and paid media agencies, including every tool the business pays for.

Why do performance agencies get a higher range?

Their delivery runs on software. SEO, PPC, analytics and CRO tools are part of the cost of the service and create client value directly, while creative agencies are labor heavy and tool light.

When should a new tool be approved?

Only when it replaces an existing tool, increases revenue capacity, reduces headcount needs, or improves pricing power. Anything else waits.

How often should software be reviewed?

Quarterly. Cancel tools used by less than 20% of the team, tools not tied to delivery or revenue, and tools that duplicate something you already pay for.
