Revenue Per Employee Benchmarks for Marketing Agencies

Ensure your agency grows revenue faster than headcount so profit doesn't collapse as you scale

One number tells you whether your team size and revenue are in balance. This is it.

Goal

Ensure your agency grows revenue faster than headcount so profit does not collapse as you scale.

Revenue per employee answers one question: how much revenue does the business generate for each full-time equivalent person on the team?

Formula
Annual Revenue ÷ Full-Time Equivalent (FTE) Team Size

Include contractors: convert to fractional FTEs based on workload.
Example: 10 hrs/week = 0.25 FTE

Target range for this OS: $150,000–$200,000 per employee. Write yours here:

Below this range typically indicates overstaffing, pricing that is too low, excess non-billable workload, or inefficient processes. Above this range reflects strong pricing discipline, high utilization, and operational leverage from systems and tooling.

Decision Rules
  • RPE is below $150,000: do not hire. Fix pricing, utilization, or team structure first.
  • RPE is $150,000–$180,000: hiring is possible but should be conservative.
  • RPE is above $180,000: you have room to scale capacity safely.
  • before any new hire: project RPE post-hire. If it drops below benchmark range, delay.
Example: $1.2M Agency
Annual Revenue $1,200,000
Team Size 7 FTE (6 W-2 + 2 contractors at 0.5 FTE each)
RPE $171,400 Healthy: hiring OK, be deliberate
Tradeoffs

Higher RPE = higher margins, more cash buffer, more resilience in downturns, but potential for burnout. Lower RPE = faster capacity growth, but higher financial risk if revenue dips. There is no perfect number, but there is a dangerous range. This OS keeps you out of it.

Agency gross income per FTE

If you buy media, printing or production for clients and rebill it, those pass-through costs inflate revenue without adding work for your team. Agency gross income (AGI) strips them out, so it is the fairer number for revenue per person in an agency that passes through spend.

  • Agency gross income = revenue − pass-through costs rebilled to clients
  • AGI per FTE = agency gross income ÷ FTE team size, with contractors as fractional FTEs

An agency with little or no pass-through spend has AGI close to revenue, so the two numbers match. An agency that runs large media budgets should track AGI per FTE against the target instead, and keep pass-throughs in their own revenue and cost accounts. Accounting for marketing agencies covers how to book them.

Worked example: the $1.2M agency, before and after a hire

The module's own example, with its figures as given above.

  • Team: 6 W-2 employees + 2 contractors at 0.5 FTE each = 6 + 1 = 7 FTE
  • RPE today: $1,200,000 ÷ 7 = $171,429, shown above as $171,400 (healthy)
  • RPE after one full-time hire at the same revenue: $1,200,000 ÷ 8 = $150,000

$150,000 is the bottom of the target range. The decision rule says project RPE before any hire and delay if it drops below range, so this hire is possible only if revenue rises with it or the role removes a bottleneck.

Questions

What is a good revenue per employee for a marketing agency?

The WGYB target is $150,000 to $200,000 per full-time equivalent. Below $120,000 is weak, $120,000 to $150,000 is acceptable only with a clear reason to hire, $150,000 to $180,000 is healthy, and $180,000 to $220,000 or more is strong.

Should contractors count in revenue per employee?

Yes, as fractional FTEs based on workload. A contractor at 10 hours a week is 0.25 FTE. Leaving them out makes the number look better than the team really is.

What is AGI per FTE?

Agency gross income per full-time equivalent: revenue minus pass-through costs, divided by FTEs. It is the better measure for agencies that rebill media or production, because pass-throughs add revenue without adding work.

Why is public revenue per employee different from the WGYB target?

Public data counts employees only and includes firms of every size, while the WGYB target counts contractors as FTEs and comes from agencies in the $300K to $3M range. Use the public figure for context and the target for decisions.
