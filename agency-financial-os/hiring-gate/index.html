When Can Your Agency Afford to Hire? The Hiring Gate

A data-driven framework so you never wonder if you're going to be OK financially

Hiring when cash is tight and margins are thin feels like relief for 30 days and pain for 12 months.

Goal

Data-driven framework to hire so you never wonder if you are going to be OK financially.

Only hire when all three conditions are true.

1
Capacity Is Truly Constrained

Your delivery team is consistently above target utilization, or client work is slipping on quality or deadlines. The team has been working 120%+ for 2+ months with no end in sight.

2
The Model Can Afford It
  • 3 months of operating cash on hand
  • Profit within benchmark range
  • Revenue per employee > $140,000/year
3
You Have a Plan for Revenue or Efficiency

The hire is tied to booked revenue, a clear pipeline, or removing a bottleneck that directly increases billable output.

If any condition fails, delay hire. All three must be true before you post a job.

Common Hiring Mistake

Hiring when cash is tight and margins are already thin. If profit % and RPE are below benchmarks and you hire anyway, you will feel relief for 30 days and pain for 12 months.

The hiring gate thresholds, checked step by step

The three conditions as numbers, how to test each one, and the module's example worked through

The hiring gate is a WGYB rule set from client work. All three conditions have to be true before you post a job: capacity is truly constrained, the financial model can afford the hire, and the hire is tied to revenue or efficiency. Two of the three are numbers you can check from your books in a few minutes.

The gate as numbers

Source: WGYB benchmark from client work (this module, with profit bands from Module 1).

ConditionThresholdHow to measure it
CapacityTeam above 120% for 2+ monthsHours worked ÷ hours available, per person, for the last two months
Cash3 months of operating cash on handCash in the bank ÷ average monthly operating costs
ProfitWithin the benchmark bandOperating profit ÷ revenue, against your band in Module 1
Revenue per employeeOver $140,000 a yearAnnual revenue ÷ FTE team size, contractors as fractional FTEs
PlanTied to revenue or efficiencyBooked revenue, a clear pipeline, or a removed bottleneck that raises billable output

Worked example: the module's $1M agency

The figures given above: a team of 3 at $1M in annual revenue, monthly revenue of $83,333, monthly costs of $60,000 and $200,000 cash on hand.

  • Months of cash: $200,000 ÷ $60,000 = 3.3 months, above the 3-month line
  • Monthly operating profit: $83,333 − $60,000 = $23,333, and $23,333 ÷ $83,333 = 28%
  • Profit band for $1M: 15% to 20%, so 28% is above the band
  • Revenue per employee: $250,000 as given in the example, above the $140,000 line
  • Capacity: the team has run at 120%+ for months, and the plan is a part-time ops manager who frees the team from admin

Every condition passes, so the decision is hire. If cash on hand had been $150,000, the same agency would hold 2.5 months ($150,000 ÷ $60,000) and the gate would say wait.

Questions

When can an agency afford to hire?

When all three conditions are true: the team has been over capacity for two months or more, the model can afford it (3 months of operating cash, profit within its band, revenue per employee over $140,000), and the hire is tied to revenue or to removing a bottleneck.

How much cash should an agency have before hiring?

At least 3 months of operating cash on hand, measured as cash in the bank divided by average monthly operating costs.

What if only two of the three conditions pass?

Delay the hire. The rule is that all three must be true. Hiring when cash is tight and margins are thin feels like relief for 30 days and pain for 12 months.

Who should the first hire be once the gate is clear?

The hiring order module covers it: remove founder bottlenecks first, stabilize delivery second, add growth roles last.
