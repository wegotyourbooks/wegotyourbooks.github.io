How the stack runs the monthly close

What each tool sends to QuickBooks, what to check each month, and one transaction followed from card to dashboard

The stack above is the setup we use with agency clients: QuickBooks Online as the single source of truth, with Gusto, Ramp and the bank feeding it and Fathom reading from it. It works because every tool sends data in one direction. This section shows what each one sends and what the monthly close checks.

What each tool sends, and what to check each month

Source: the Agency Financial OS, Module 9, and the monthly close described in Module 10.

ToolSends to QuickBooksMonthly check
Business bankEvery deposit and payment, by bank feedReconciled to the statement balance
RampCard spend with receipts and categoriesEvery transaction coded, receipts attached for large or unusual items
GustoPayroll journal entriesPayroll mapped to the right accounts, delivery kept apart from overhead
QuickBooks Online invoicingInvoices, payments and recurring billingReceivables aged and followed up
FathomReads from QuickBooks; sends nothing backDashboard refreshed after the close, KPIs reviewed

Worked example: one card purchase, from swipe to dashboard

  • A designer buys a stock image subscription on a Ramp virtual card and snaps the receipt
  • Ramp codes it to the software account and syncs it to QuickBooks with the receipt attached
  • At month-end, the card account is reconciled in QuickBooks along with every other account
  • Fathom refreshes from QuickBooks, and the purchase shows up in software spend as a percentage of revenue
  • The quarterly software review checks whether the tool passes the new tool rule in software spend

No one re-enters the purchase, and no reimbursement is needed, which is the point of putting card spend on Ramp.

The close, in the order the stack needs it

The order matters, because each step reads from the one before it. Source: the Agency Financial OS, Module 10, Phases 1, 2 and 7.

  1. Bank and card feeds complete through the last day of the month
  2. Every account reconciled to its statement, bank and Ramp cards included
  3. Uncategorized and miscategorized transactions cleaned up
  4. Payroll and contractor payments checked against the right accounts, delivery apart from overhead
  5. Revenue checked against client invoices, so income maps to the right client and service
  6. The P&L closed by the set date, then Fathom refreshed and the KPIs reviewed

Skip a step and the dashboard still updates, but it shows the wrong number with the same confidence as the right one. That is the main risk of a connected stack: errors travel as fast as data.

Questions

What accounting software should a marketing agency use?

QuickBooks Online. It is standard with CPAs and buyers, supports classes and locations for agency reporting, and the rest of the stack integrates into it.

Why keep one source of truth?

When every tool feeds QuickBooks and reporting reads from it, the dashboard, the tax return and the forecast all start from the same numbers. Two sources mean two answers to the same question.

What does Fathom add on top of QuickBooks?

Dashboards, KPIs and cash flow forecasting that read directly from QuickBooks Online, built for owners who are not accountants.

Which business bank works best with this stack?

Mercury or another modern business bank. The module's reasons: faster access, cleaner integrations with QuickBooks, and an easy way to keep operating and reserve accounts separate.

Do I need every tool on day one?

QuickBooks Online and a business bank come first. Add payroll, spend controls and dashboards as the team and the volume of transactions grow.
