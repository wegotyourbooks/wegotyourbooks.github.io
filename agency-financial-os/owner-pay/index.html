How Much Should an Agency Owner Pay Themselves?

A clear picture of how much you should pay yourself as you grow

Most agency owners are either chronically underpaid or pulling money out reactively. Neither is a system.

Goal

Have a clear picture of how much you should pay yourself as you continue to grow.

Benchmarks: Owner Compensation (Salary + Distributions)

Revenue RangeOwner CompensationYour Number
$300K–$600K$70K–$110K
$600K–$1.2M$110K–$170K
$1.2M–$3M$150K–$300K
Decision Rules
  • profit is < 10%: cap compensation
  • profit meets target: pay within range
  • profit exceeds target: distribute surplus or reinvest into the business
Tradeoffs

More owner pay reduces growth capital but rewards the founder. Less owner pay increases reinvestment capacity but limits present reward to owner.

Owner pay as a share of revenue

What the bands mean in percentages, how to split salary and distributions, and a worked example

The owner pay bands above are a WGYB benchmark from client work, and they cover total owner compensation: salary plus distributions. They are tied to profit. When profit is under 10%, compensation gets capped. When profit meets the target, you pay within the band. When profit beats the target, the surplus is distributed or reinvested.

Owner pay as a share of revenue at the band edges

Source: WGYB benchmark from client work (the bands above). The percentages divide each band's pay by the revenue at that edge.

Revenue bandOwner compensationLow end as % of revenueHigh end as % of revenue
$300K to $600K$70K to $110K$70,000 ÷ $300,000 = 23%$110,000 ÷ $600,000 = 18%
$600K to $1.2M$110K to $170K$110,000 ÷ $600,000 = 18%$170,000 ÷ $1,200,000 = 14%
$1.2M to $3M$150K to $300K$150,000 ÷ $1,200,000 = 13%$300,000 ÷ $3,000,000 = 10%

Owner pay grows in dollars and shrinks as a share of revenue. The agency gets more profitable as it grows, so the owner can take home more while a smaller slice of each dollar goes to them.

Worked example: the $1M agency from the Hiring Gate

The Hiring Gate module's agency runs $1M in revenue with a 28% monthly operating profit ($83,333 in, $60,000 out, as given there).

  • Band for $1M in revenue: $600K to $1.2M, so owner compensation of $110K to $170K
  • Profit target for that band: 15% to 20%; this agency runs 28%, so profit exceeds target
  • Decision rule: pay within the band, then distribute the surplus or reinvest it
  • Annual operating profit: $23,333 × 12 = $279,996, about $280,000, of which the part above 20% of revenue is $280,000 − $200,000 = $80,000

That $80,000 is the room for a distribution or a reinvestment decision, after the owner's in-band pay is already counted as a cost.

Questions

How much should an agency owner pay themselves?

On the WGYB benchmark, total owner compensation runs $70K to $110K at $300K to $600K in revenue, $110K to $170K at $600K to $1.2M, and $150K to $300K at $1.2M to $3M, as long as profit is on target.

Should I pay myself salary or distributions?

The benchmark counts both together. The split between them is a tax question, especially for S-corp owners, so set it with your CPA. The tax planning benchmarks show the effective rates to watch.

What if profit is below 10%?

The decision rule caps owner compensation until profit recovers. Paying yourself the full band out of a thin margin usually means borrowing from the cash the business needs for payroll.

Does owner pay count in team cost?

The team cost benchmark is shown both ways, excluding and including owner pay. Including it, people cost runs 45% to 60% of revenue at $300K to $600K, falling to 30% to 45% at $1.2M to $3M.
