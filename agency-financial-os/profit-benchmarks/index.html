Agency Profit Margin Benchmarks by Revenue Band

Understand what profit margins are industry standard by revenue bands

Most agency owners don't have a profit target. They have a revenue goal and a hope that something's left over.

Goal

Understand what profit margins are industry standard by revenue bands.

Benchmarks: Operating Profit After Payroll, Before Owner Distributions

Revenue RangeOperating Profit %Your Number
$300K–$600K10–15%
$600K–$1.2M15–20%
$1.2M–$3M18–25%
Decision Rules
  • profit is below range: freeze hiring, reduce software, double down on business development
  • profit is within range: maintain pace
  • profit is above range: increase owner distributions or reinvest
Tradeoffs

Higher profit = slower growth, lower risk. Lower profit = faster growth, higher stress.

Profit margin benchmarks in dollars, and by agency type

How to measure your number, what each band looks like in dollars, and where public data sits next to it

The bands above are a WGYB benchmark from client work: operating profit after payroll and before owner distributions. To compare your agency, take revenue for the last twelve months, subtract every cost except owner distributions, and divide by revenue. Use the same twelve months for both numbers, and count contractors and your own salary as costs.

The bands in dollars

Each band applied to the midpoint of its revenue range. Source: WGYB benchmark from client work (the bands above); the dollar figures are that benchmark multiplied out.

Worked example: the $1M agency from the Hiring Gate

The Hiring Gate module uses an agency at $1M in annual revenue. Its figures, as given there: monthly revenue of $83,333 and monthly costs of $60,000.

  • Monthly operating profit: $83,333 − $60,000 = $23,333
  • Operating profit margin: $23,333 ÷ $83,333 = 28%
  • Annual operating profit: $23,333 × 12 = $279,996, about $280,000
  • Band for $1M in revenue: $600K to $1.2M, so 15% to 20%, or $150,000 to $200,000 at $1M
  • Distance above the top of the band: $280,000 − $200,000 = $80,000

The margin is above range, so the decision rule says increase owner distributions or reinvest. The same agency at 13% would sit below the band, and the rule would be to freeze hiring, cut software and push business development.

Questions

What is a good profit margin for a marketing agency?

On the WGYB benchmark, operating profit after payroll and before owner distributions runs 10% to 15% at $300K to $600K in revenue, 15% to 20% at $600K to $1.2M, and 18% to 25% at $1.2M to $3M.

Does owner pay count before or after profit?

Your salary counts as a cost before profit. Owner distributions come out of profit, after it is measured. That keeps the margin comparable whether you pay yourself mostly in salary or mostly in distributions.

Why does the target margin rise with revenue?

Fixed costs such as software, rent and admin roles spread over more revenue as the agency grows, so the same pricing leaves more profit per dollar. The bands rise from 10% to 15% at the smallest size to 18% to 25% at $1.2M to $3M.

What should I do if my margin is below the band?

The decision rule is to freeze hiring, reduce software and put more time into business development until profit is back in range. The team cost and software spend benchmarks show where the margin usually went.
