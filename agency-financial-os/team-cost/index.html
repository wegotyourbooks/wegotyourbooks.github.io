Be crystal clear if you are overstaffed, underpriced, and how to grow without losing profit

How to measure people cost, what the bands mean in dollars, and where public payroll data sits next to them

The team cost bands above are a WGYB benchmark from client work. People cost means W-2 payroll plus contractors, as a percentage of revenue, shown excluding and including owner pay. Measure it over the last twelve months: add gross wages, employer payroll taxes, benefits and every contractor payment for delivery or operations, then divide by revenue for the same months.

Worked example: one more hire at $1M

Take the $1M agency and assume people cost sits at the middle of its band, 30% excluding owner pay, which is $1,000,000 × 30% = $300,000. A U.S. delivery role costs $50K to $85K a year on the Hiring Optimization module's ranges.

  • After a $50,000 hire: $300,000 + $50,000 = $350,000, or 35% of revenue, the top of the band
  • After an $85,000 hire: $300,000 + $85,000 = $385,000, or 38.5% of revenue, above the band and close to the 40% freeze line

Unless revenue grows with the hire, the same person can keep the agency in range or push it out. That is why the hiring gate ties every hire to booked revenue or a removed bottleneck.

Questions

What percentage of revenue should an agency spend on payroll?

On the WGYB benchmark, people cost excluding owner pay runs 30% to 40% at $300K to $600K, 25% to 35% at $600K to $1.2M, and 22% to 32% at $1.2M to $3M.

Do contractors count as team cost?

Yes. The benchmark counts W-2 payroll and contractors together, because a freelancer doing client work costs margin the same way an employee does.

What does it mean when payroll is over 40%?

The decision rule is to freeze hiring and look at compensation, pricing and utilization. Over 40%, the agency is usually overstaffed for its revenue or underpriced for its work.

Can team cost be too low?

Under 25%, the rule flags a risk of burnout and bottlenecks. Margins look strong, but the founder or a few people carry the load, and that usually shows up later as turnover or slipping delivery.
