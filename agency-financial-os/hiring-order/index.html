Who to Hire First in a Marketing Agency

Who to hire first, how to structure the role, how to control risk

The highest-ROI hiring sequence is always the same: remove founder bottlenecks first, stabilize delivery second, add growth roles last.

Goal

Hire the right role, at the right time, in the right way, without damaging margins or delivery quality.

This module assumes you already meet the financial conditions to hire (see Hiring Gate). Now the question is who to hire first, how to structure the role, and how to control risk.

Worked example: a delivery hire at the $1.2M agency

The Revenue Per Employee module's agency runs $1,200,000 on 7 FTE. A step 2 delivery hire in the U.S. costs $50K to $85K on the range above.

  • Revenue per employee today: $1,200,000 ÷ 7 = $171,429
  • After a full-time delivery hire, revenue unchanged: $1,200,000 ÷ 8 = $150,000, the bottom of the target range
  • Revenue needed to hold today's level with 8 FTE: $171,429 × 8 = $1,371,432, about $171,000 more a year

A part-time or 1099 delivery role at 0.5 FTE moves the number less: $1,200,000 ÷ 7.5 = $160,000. That is why the module suggests 1099 for variable delivery work and W-2 once the work is stable and recurring.

Questions

Who should an agency hire first?

Admin support that takes scheduling, inbox, invoicing follow-ups and CRM updates off the founder. Then delivery support, then a project or operations manager, and sales roles last, once delivery is stable.

Should the first hire be W-2 or 1099?

W-2 fits ongoing work that is core to delivery, where you control the schedule and tools. 1099 fits variable workloads, specialized skills, and roles you are still testing.

Which roles work well offshore?

The module lists admin, QA, design production, SEO execution and reporting. Client-facing strategy, account management, sales and senior leadership work better in the U.S.

When should an agency hire its first salesperson?

Only when delivery is stable, admin is handled and operations are organized. A sales hire into an unstable delivery team sells work the team can't deliver well.
