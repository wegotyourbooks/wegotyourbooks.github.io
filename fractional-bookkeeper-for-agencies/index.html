What a fractional bookkeeper does for an agency

A part-time bookkeeper who closes your books every month, without a full-time salary. Here's what the role covers, how it's priced, and when an agency needs a fractional CFO instead.

  • Part-time by design
  • Monthly close
  • Last month is the horizon

The short version

  • A fractional bookkeeper keeps your books on a part-time basis: transactions coded, accounts reconciled, the month closed.
  • The horizon is last month. A fractional CFO looks at next quarter: forecasts, owner pay, the next hire.
  • Pricing is usually a fixed monthly fee set by transaction volume and how many accounts there are to reconcile.
  • Most agencies need the bookkeeper first. A forecast built on books that don't close is a guess.

What "fractional" means

Fractional means you get a fraction of a person's time, not a fraction of the work. The books still close every month; you just don't pay for forty hours a week to do it.

For an agency doing a few hundred thousand to a few million in revenue, the bookkeeping rarely fills a full-time job. A full-time hire either sits idle part of the month or gets handed admin that isn't bookkeeping. A fractional bookkeeper does the books on a schedule and stops there.

The arrangement works when three things are true: the data flows in on its own through bank and card feeds, there is a set close date, and someone owns the questions the bookkeeper can't answer from the data.

What a good monthly close includes

The test of a bookkeeper is the close: what gets done, and by when. These are the steps a sound close covers, in the order they depend on each other.

  1. 1

    Every transaction coded

    Bank, card and processor activity categorized to a chart of accounts that splits delivery cost from overhead.

  2. 2

    Every account reconciled

    Each bank, card and processor account matched to its statement balance, so the books agree with the bank.

  3. 3

    Payroll and contractors mapped

    Payroll entered from the provider's summaries, contractors coded to delivery or overhead depending on the work.

  4. 4

    A P&L on a set date

    The month closed and the profit and loss statement delivered on a date written down in advance, not whenever it gets done.

  5. 5

    Questions batched

    Anything the bookkeeper can't work out from the data goes to you in one short list, not a drip of emails.

Fractional bookkeeper vs fractional CFO

The two roles look at different time horizons. A bookkeeper records last month. A fractional CFO works on next quarter. A CPA, the third role most agencies already have, files last year.

When an agency needs which

A fractional bookkeeper is enough when

  • The main problem is that the books are behind or don't reconcile
  • Your CPA keeps asking for the same cleanup at year-end
  • You can't say what last month's profit was without logging in and guessing

Add a fractional CFO when

  • The books close on time, and the open questions are about the future: pricing, hiring, owner pay
  • You're about to make a decision you'd normally make on instinct, such as a hire or a new offer
  • Revenue is growing but profit isn't, and you can't see which lever to pull

The benchmarks a CFO measures your books against are published: profit margins, owner pay and the hiring gate. They only mean something once the books underneath them are closed.

What to ask before you hire one

  • What date does the month close? A real answer names a business day of the month.
  • What's out of scope? Tax filing, invoicing your clients and paying your vendors are often separate. Get it in writing.
  • How do questions reach me? One batched list a month costs you minutes. A steady stream costs you hours.
  • Do you know agency books? Pass-through costs, retainers billed ahead and contractors in delivery cost trip up generalists. Accounting for marketing agencies covers why.
  • Who answers my CPA? The bookkeeper should handle the CPA's data requests directly, so year-end isn't a project for you.

Questions

The ones people actually ask on the first call

What is a fractional bookkeeper?

A bookkeeper who works for you part-time, usually on a fixed monthly scope: coding transactions, reconciling every account and closing the books each month, without the cost of a full-time hire.

What's the difference between a fractional bookkeeper and a fractional CFO?

The time horizon. A fractional bookkeeper records and closes last month. A fractional CFO uses those closed books to plan next quarter: forecasts, pricing, owner pay and hiring.

How much does a fractional bookkeeper cost?

It depends on the pricing model and the volume of transactions. Ours is a fixed monthly fee: Starter is $500 to $800 a month, Growth $1,500 and Scale $2,500, with a 6-month minimum.

Does a fractional bookkeeper do my taxes?

Usually not, and ours doesn't. A bookkeeper keeps clean books through the year and answers your CPA's questions. The CPA prepares and files the return.

What should a fractional bookkeeper deliver each month?

Every account reconciled, the month closed with a P&L on a date set in advance, and one short list of the questions the data couldn't answer. If you can't name the close date, ask for one.

Do I need a fractional CFO if I have a bookkeeper?

Only once the books close on time and the questions you're stuck on are about the future. If the books are behind, fix that first, because a forecast built on them won't hold.
